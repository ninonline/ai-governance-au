# AI governance templates for Australian professional firms

Practical documents for a firm that has started using AI in client work and now has to govern it. Written for financial advice practices, accounting practices and small law firms in Australia. Free to use and adapt under CC BY 4.0.

These are working documents, not legal advice. A firm should have its own adviser review anything it adopts.

## What is here

| Document | Use it when | Status |
|---|---|---|
| AI use policy, financial advice practice | Staff are using AI tools and the practice has no policy | Drafting, due September 2026 |
| AI use case risk assessment | A new AI use is proposed and someone must decide whether it proceeds | Drafting, due September 2026 |
| AI tool register | No one can say which AI tools the firm actually uses | Planned |
| AI use policy, accounting practice | The same need, in a tax and accounting practice | Planned |
| How to write an AI use policy for a small firm | You want the reasoning behind the templates before you adopt one | Planned |
| Automated decision transparency, what to publish | Preparing for the privacy policy obligation that starts on 10 December 2026 | Planned, after the OAIC guidance is published |
| Mapping to the six essential practices | A client, insurer or regulator asks which framework the firm follows | Planned |

Nothing is listed here that is not being written. Each document is published when it is good enough to adopt, rather than as a placeholder.

## How to use a template

Read the guide first, then copy the template into your own document. Replace every field in square brackets. Delete any clause that does not describe your firm. Keep the version and review date. An unreviewed policy stops being true. Complete a risk assessment for each AI use before it goes live, and keep the completed form with the file.

## What these documents are built on

Australia has no AI Act, and none is planned. AI use is governed by existing law and by voluntary guidance. These templates are written against five sources.

| Source | What it is |
|---|---|
| Guidance for AI Adoption, National AI Centre, October 2025 | Six essential practices, which evolved the earlier ten guardrails |
| Voluntary AI Safety Standard, September 2024 | Ten guardrails, still published and still cited in contracts and risk registers |
| Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), Australian Privacy Principles | A transparency obligation for automated decisions starts on 10 December 2026 |
| ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | The international standard for an AI management system |
| ASIC Report 798, Beware the gap, October 2024 | The regulator's stated expectations of financial services licensees using AI |

Each template names the sources it relies on in its front matter, with the date the source was checked.

## Versions

Documents use semantic versions. A change to a clause raises the minor version. A change that alters what a firm must do raises the major version. The changelog records what changed and why.
